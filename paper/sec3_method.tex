\section{Estimation method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Observation model}\label{sec:obs}
Pass $i$ is a record of vertical axle-box acceleration over \NumRevs{} wheel revolutions, so its
length is fixed in space, $L = \NumRevs\cdot 2\pi r_0$, and its duration $T_i = L/V_i$ depends on the
pass speed $V_i$. The DFT coefficient $c_{ik}$ at bin $k$ belongs to the frequency $f_{ik} = k/T_i$
and to the same wavenumber $\gamma_k = 2\pi k/L$ on every pass. The analysis band is fixed in
frequency.

\paragraph{Roughness model.} Rail roughness enters at two levels. Its \emph{spectrum} is
\begin{equation}
  G(\gamma) = G_\mathrm{ISO}(\gamma)\,g_{s(\gamma)},
\end{equation}
a one-sided power spectral density in angular wavenumber $\gamma$, where $G_\mathrm{ISO}$ is a fixed
reference shape and $g_s$ is a free level in sub-band $s$, with $K$ sub-bands per one-third octave. The
levels are unknown, shared by all passes of one monitoring period, and estimated with the globals; the
method never measures roughness. The \emph{realisation} on each pass is random: a zero-mean
stationary Gaussian profile with spectrum $G$, drawn independently for every pass, because successive
passes run over different stretches of track or with different lateral wheel positions. All wheels of
one pass run over the same rail profile, shifted by their distance along the wagon; this is what produces
the phase factors between wheels in~\eqref{eq:H}. Passes that repeat over the same section share part
of the realisation; Section~\ref{sec:res-repeated} quantifies the effect and gives the robust standard
error used for that case. The data generator, the likelihood~\eqref{eq:whittle}, the expected Fisher
information and every Monte Carlo study of Section~\ref{sec:results} use this model: one level vector
per period (drawn with a 3~dB spread around the reference in the simulations), an independent Gaussian
realisation per pass, and the same profile under all wheels of a pass.

\paragraph{Reference spectrum.} $G_\mathrm{ISO}$ has the shape of the rail roughness limit for the
reference track of ISO~3095:2013, Clause~6.2.5, Figure~2~\citep{iso3095}, the edition used here (it was
withdrawn and replaced by ISO~3095:2025 in September 2025): one-third-octave roughness
levels $L_r$ in dB re 1~\textmu m at \ISONBands{} centre wavelengths $\lambda_c$ from \ISOLambdaMax~m to
\ISOLambdaMinMM~mm. Each band level is converted to a mean-square roughness
$10^{-12}\,10^{L_r/10}$~m$^2$ and divided by the band width in angular wavenumber,
$\gamma_c(2^{1/6}-2^{-1/6})$ with $\gamma_c = 2\pi/\lambda_c$, to give the density at $\gamma_c$ in
m$^2$/(rad/m); $G_\mathrm{ISO}$ interpolates these densities linearly in $\log G$ against $\log\gamma$,
so that its band integrals reproduce the tabulated levels. Over 60--110~km/h the analysis band
\BandLo--\BandHi~Hz covers wavelengths from \LambdaMinCM~cm to \LambdaMaxM~m; bins whose wavenumber
lies outside the tabulated range are not used. Only the shape matters, since the levels $g_s$ are free.

\paragraph{Payload and noise.} The axle load of each pass, $W_i$, is a nuisance with a Gaussian prior on
$\log W_i$ centred on a weigh-in-motion reading with standard deviation \WIMsigmaPct\%. The sensor
noise is white with level $e_i$. It is estimated for each pass as the mean $|c_{ik}|^2$ over the
\NoiseLo--\NoiseHi~Hz bins of the same record, above the analysis band and below the anti-alias limit,
where the response to roughness is negligible; with \NoiseBinsMin--\NoiseBinsMax{} bins per record its
relative standard error is at most about \NoiseRelSEMax\%. The estimate is used as a fixed plug-in value
and its uncertainty is not propagated; at the signal-to-noise ratios studied the noise is small against
the signal near the support resonance, which carries the information on $k_{B0}$. The estimator can
instead profile $e_i$ jointly from the in-band and noise-only bins; this option was not used for the
results reported. The globals $\bm\theta$ are the health parameters $(k_{B0}, c_B)$, optionally with
track parameters such as $k_f$ and $k_{H0}$.

Under this model the coefficients are approximately independent complex Gaussians with variance
\begin{equation}
  v_{ik} = |H(f_{ik};\bm\theta, W_i, V_i)|^2\, 2G(\gamma_k)\,\Delta\gamma + e_i,
  \label{eq:binvar}
\end{equation}
and the negative log-likelihood over $N$ passes is the multi-pass Whittle form~\citep{whittle1953}
\begin{equation}
  \ell(\bm\theta, \bm g, \bm W) = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_k\left[\log v_{ik} + \frac{|c_{ik}|^2}{v_{ik}}\right]
  + \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(\log W_i - \log\hat W_i)^2}{2\sigma_W^2}.
  \label{eq:whittle}
\end{equation}
The same structure, with the modal-force spectrum per band as the nuisance, underlies Bayesian
FFT operational modal analysis~\citep{au2011}.

\paragraph{Why speed diversity identifies the support.} Roughness is fixed in wavenumber, while the
resonance of the axlebox on its support is fixed in frequency. A pass at speed $V_i$ maps
wavenumber $\gamma$ to frequency $V_i\gamma/2\pi$, so passes at different speeds see the same
roughness sub-band through different parts of $|H|^2$. With a single speed, a change of $|H|^2$ and
a change of roughness level are indistinguishable sub-band by sub-band; with a spread of speeds
they separate. Figure~\ref{fig:crb-speed} shows, for an earlier configuration with a single wheelset on
a rail oscillator, the Cram\'er--Rao bound of $k_{B0}$ falling with the speed spread for several sub-band
resolutions $K$.

\subsection{Debiased likelihood for multi-wheel cross terms}\label{sec:debias}
Equation~\eqref{eq:binvar} samples $|H|^2G$ at the bin. The expected periodogram of a finite
record is instead the spectrum convolved with the Fej\'er kernel $T\,\mathrm{sinc}^2((f-f_k)T)$.
The two agree when the spectrum is smooth on the scale of one bin, as for a single wheelset. They
do not agree once the multi-wheel phase factors in~\eqref{eq:H} put ripples of period $V/(d_k-d_j)$
close to the bin width. Following Sykulski et al.~\citep{sykulski2019}, we keep the Whittle form but
replace $v_{ik}$ by the exact expected periodogram,
\begin{equation}
  v_{ik} = \sum_m \frac{\mathrm{sinc}^2\!\big((f_m - f_{ik})T_i\big)}{M}\,
           |H(f_m)|^2\,2G(\gamma_m)\,\Delta\gamma + e_i,
  \label{eq:debiased}
\end{equation}
with the sum over a grid $M=\KernelFine$ times finer than the bins, truncated at $\pm\KernelHalfWidth$ bins
(a narrower truncation at $\pm30$ bins leaves a measurable bias; Section~\ref{sec:res-bias}). On the
simulated 12-wheel setup of~\citep{carrigan2023} (an earlier configuration in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:results}), the bin-sampled model differs from the mean of
\DebiasRecords{} periodograms by an rms log-ratio of \DebiasBin{}; the debiased model differs by
\DebiasDeb{}, against \DebiasNoise{} expected from sampling alone (Fig.~\ref{fig:debias}).

\subsection{Profiling and optimisation}\label{sec:profile}
For given globals $\bm\theta$, the nuisances $(\log\bm g, \log\bm W)$ are profiled out by Fisher
scoring, warm-started from the previous evaluation. The globals are then found by Nelder--Mead on
the profile likelihood. With the debiased variance~\eqref{eq:debiased} the kernel mixes
neighbouring roughness sub-bands. The default scoring step attributes each bin's variance to its
own sub-band; the exact step uses the full derivative $\partial v_{ik}/\partial\log g_s$ over all
sub-bands and the corresponding Fisher matrix, so that its fixed point is the exact profile maximum.
The default step is cheaper but its fixed point differs slightly from the exact maximum. Scoring stops
when the largest step in any $\log g_s$ or $\log W_i$ is below $10^{-7}$ (at most 80 iterations); the
outer Nelder--Mead search stops at a tolerance of $10^{-4}$ in the log globals and $10^{-3}$ in the
negative log-likelihood. On the ten seed-123 datasets of the headline study (pinned headline configuration), the exact step changes the
estimate of $k_{B0}$ by at most \ScoreExactMaxDiff{} percentage points (mean \ScoreExactMeanDiff) and
the standard error by a factor of \ScoreExactSERatio{} on average (Section~\ref{sec:res-bias}).

The transfer functions are computed directly from the finite-element and rail models (no
surrogate): a table of the coupled components $G_k(f;\bm\theta,W)$ over payload nodes, built from an
exact modal decomposition of the wheelset for each trial $\bm\theta$, is interpolated in $\log W$
and frequency.

\subsection{Uncertainty}\label{sec:uncertainty}
Wald intervals use the observed information of the profile likelihood (numerical Hessian).
Profile-likelihood intervals use the $\chi^2_1$ cut, re-optimising the other globals and all
nuisances. The Cram\'er--Rao bound (CRB) is the inverse expected Fisher information with every
nuisance free and the weigh-in-motion prior included. When records repeat over the same section,
the independence assumed in~\eqref{eq:whittle} fails across passes; the variance is then taken
from a sandwich estimator clustered on DFT bin (the same wavenumber on every pass).
Where the Wald intervals under-cover in simulation (Section~\ref{sec:res-headline}), they are
simulation-calibrated for the design: the mean bias of $\log\hat k_{B0}$ found by simulation is
subtracted and the standard error is multiplied by the simulated ratio of RMSE to standard error,
checked on held-out simulation seeds.

\subsection{Measurement requirements}\label{sec:requirements}
The method uses the axle-box accelerometer and the speed and load data that an instrumented freight
wagon for track monitoring typically records, but it places requirements on them.
\emph{Sensor and sampling:} a vertical accelerometer on the axlebox with a flat response over at least
\BandLo--\NoiseHi~Hz (the analysis band and the noise band), sampled at \SampleRate~Hz or more with an
anti-alias filter above the noise band. \emph{Calibration:} a constant sensitivity error cancels in the
relative change of two periods measured with the same sensor, but a change of sensitivity between periods
does not, so the sensor must be calibrated or checked at the start of each period; the absolute level of
$k_{B0}$ additionally needs the absolute calibration. \emph{Speed:} the speed of each pass must be known,
since it sets the mapping between wavenumber and frequency, and approximately constant over the record;
records with braking, traction transients or speed changes of more than a few per cent would be
excluded. \emph{Record alignment:} each record covers a fixed distance (\NumRevs{} wheel revolutions,
\RecordLength~m), so it must be cut on a distance or wheel-angle reference (tachometer or odometry), not
on a fixed duration. \emph{Bearing orders:} varying-compliance vibration of the roller bearing puts
lines at the outer-race roller-pass order, about \BearingOrderOR{} times the rotation frequency
(\BearingOrderORLo--\BearingOrderORHi{} over the assumed roller count; a non-integer order), and at its
harmonics, inside the analysis band~\citep{zheng2025}. Like roughness, these lines are tied to the wheel
angle, so they sit at fixed bins of the angle-synchronous record (only approximately, since cage slip
smears them by a few per cent); they are therefore confounded with the sub-band roughness levels, not
with the support resonance, and can be notched at the known bearing orders if needed. \emph{Wheelset
damping:} the modal damping of the wheelset dominates the error of the change under model errors
(Section~\ref{sec:res-budget}), so it should be measured, for example by an impact-hammer test on the
wheelset, rather than assumed. \emph{Payload:} an axle load per pass from weigh-in-motion or from the suspension
deflection, with an error of about \WIMsigmaPct\%, both for the likelihood and to assign each pass to
its payload band. \emph{Record selection:} records over plain line with no joints, welds,
switches, crossings or bridges, which the stationary roughness model does not represent, and passes
with the same instrumented wheelset and sensor in both periods. No sensor beyond what such a wagon
carries is needed, but the record handling above is part of the method.

\subsection{Relative change and detection}\label{sec:relative}
The monitoring output is the change of support stiffness between a baseline period and a later
period of the same vehicle and sensor, each with $N$ passes in the same payload band. Two bands are used:
empty (axle load 5.5--7~t) and laden (20--22.5~t); in each, the estimator uses the band's primary
stiffness and damping (Table~\ref{tab:table1_parameters}), and the weigh-in-motion payload assigns each
pass to its band. Pooling passes over the whole payload range requires a payload-dependent model of the
primary suspension (Section~\ref{sec:res-primary}). The change is

\begin{equation}
  D = \log\hat k_{B0}^{\,\mathrm{later}} - \log\hat k_{B0}^{\,\mathrm{base}},
\end{equation}
reported as $\Delta k_{B0}/k_{B0} = \exp(D)-1$. A misspecified model parameter biases $\log\hat k_{B0}$
in both periods; to first order the common part cancels, and the remainder scales with the change
itself. We therefore use the interval
\begin{equation}
  D \pm 1.645\sqrt{\mathrm{se}_\mathrm{base}^2 + \mathrm{se}_\mathrm{later}^2 + (\kappa D)^2},
  \label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is the model-form gain uncertainty: the root sum of squares, over the uncertain model
parameters, of the gain $\partial(\hat D/D)/\partial\log(\text{parameter})$ times that parameter's
$1\sigma$ log range. Foundation stiffness can drift between periods (seasonal pad stiffness), so the
default fit re-estimates $k_f$ and $k_{H0}$ in each period together with $(k_{B0}, c_B)$.

A loss is declared when $D < -1.645\,s_0$, where $s_0$ is the standard deviation of $D$ with no
change (5\% false-alarm rate, one-sided). The minimum detectable change (MDC) is the true decrease
detected with probability 0.9, $\mathrm{MDC} = (1.645 + 1.282)\,s_0$ without bias; with a bias in
$D$ it is found by interpolating the bias against the true change.

\subsection{Model-form bias}\label{sec:pseudotrue}
The large-sample bias under a misspecified model is the pseudo-true parameter: the minimiser of the
expected value of~\eqref{eq:whittle} when the periodograms are replaced by their expectations under
the true model. It is computed by the same profiled fit with $|c_{ik}|^2$ replaced by the true
$v_{ik}$, and was checked against full Monte Carlo fits. A first-order (score) approximation was
found to fail for large parameter errors and is not used.

\subsection{Implementation and use of AI}\label{sec:implementation}
The simulation and estimation code was developed with the assistance of an AI coding tool, Claude Code
(developer: Anthropic; model: Claude Opus 5.5). It was used to write the code (the finite-element and
rail models, the simulation and estimation scripts, and the scripts that generate tables, figures and
the numbers quoted in the text), to assist the literature search, and to draft and edit the manuscript
text. All code and text were reviewed, tested and
verified by the authors: unit tests cover the finite-element and rail models, the model is verified
against closed-form solutions (Table~\ref{tab:table2_verification}), and every result in this paper is
regenerated by script from stored, version-controlled result files.
