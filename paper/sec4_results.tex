\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

The results are organised in two parts. \emph{Verification} establishes that the estimator does what
it claims under controlled conditions: the reference model against closed-form solutions
(Table~\ref{tab:table2_verification}); the debiased likelihood against simulated periodograms
(Fig.~\ref{fig:debias}); identifiability through the Cram\'er--Rao bound and its dependence on speed
spread (Section~\ref{sec:res-speed}); bias, scatter and interval coverage in a five-seed Monte Carlo
study (Section~\ref{sec:res-headline}) with a diagnosis of the residual bias
(Section~\ref{sec:res-bias}); and robustness to model errors, to the parameter ranges of the
representative vehicle and to nonlinear contact (Sections~\ref{sec:res-modelform},
\ref{sec:res-sweep} and~\ref{sec:res-inverse-crime}). \emph{Demonstration} applies the method to the
monitoring task: detecting a change of support stiffness between periods
(Section~\ref{sec:res-relative}), what that detection limit means for bearing clearance growth
(Section~\ref{sec:res-health}), false alarms under track drift (Section~\ref{sec:res-drift}), and
practical variants of the measurement (Sections~\ref{sec:res-wheel}--\ref{sec:res-p2}).

All results are synthetic: data are generated by the reference model of Section~\ref{sec:model} with
the roughness, speed, payload and noise model of Section~\ref{sec:obs}, and fitted with the estimator of
Section~\ref{sec:method}. The default design is $N=50$ passes with speeds uniform on 60--110~km/h,
payloads uniform on \PayloadMin--\PayloadMax~t with a \WIMsigmaPct\% weigh-in-motion error, four
rail-coupled wheels, and $K=16$ roughness levels per one-third octave drawn with a 3~dB spread. The
frozen estimator uses the debiased likelihood with a $\pm\KernelHalfWidth$-bin kernel, own-sub-band
scoring, $K=16$ and a fixed load exponent. Unless stated otherwise, results are for the representative wagon of Section~\ref{sec:model} with the
values of Table~\ref{tab:table1_parameters}. The headline Monte Carlo study was started before the
contact stiffness was computed from Hertz theory and the primary damping taken from the vehicle source,
and uses $k_{H0} = 1.4$~GN/m and $c_p = 10$~kN\,s/m; both lie within the swept ranges of
Table~\ref{tab:table15_sweep}, where they change the bound by a few hundredths of a percentage point.
Three studies were run with an earlier configuration (a metro wheel layout, BS113A rail and a
$\pm30$-bin kernel) and are reported for their qualitative conclusions only: the bias diagnosis
(Section~\ref{sec:res-bias}), the multi-wheel Monte Carlo (Fig.~\ref{fig:mwbias}), the gain
uncertainty of relative changes and its replicates (Section~\ref{sec:res-relative}). Sections~\ref{sec:res-speed}, \ref{sec:res-wheel}
and~\ref{sec:res-repeated} use a single wheelset on a rail oscillator, whose transfer function is
smooth on the bin scale, and Section~\ref{sec:res-p2} uses the setup of~\citep{carrigan2023}.

\subsection{Headline Monte Carlo}\label{sec:res-headline}
Table~\ref{tab:table3_headline} summarises \HLN{} datasets per noise level (five seeds of ten). At
20~dB the estimate of $k_{B0}$ has a bias of \HLTwentyBias\,$\pm$\,\HLTwentyBiasSE\% and an RMSE of
\HLTwentyRMSE\%, \HLTwentyRatioCRB{} times the Cram\'er--Rao bound; Wald 90\% intervals cover the truth
in \HLTwentyCov{} of datasets [\HLTwentyCovLo, \HLTwentyCovHi]. At 40~dB the bias is
\HLFortyBias\,$\pm$\,\HLFortyBiasSE\%, the RMSE \HLFortyRMSE\% (\HLFortyRatioCRB{} times the bound)
and the Wald coverage \HLFortyCov{} [\HLFortyCovLo, \HLFortyCovHi]. The damping $c_B$ is less well
determined and biased by \HLTwentycBBias\% and \HLFortycBBias\%. Section~\ref{sec:res-bias} reports
what was done to explain the residual bias and excess scatter.

\paragraph{Simulation-calibrated intervals.} Because the 40~dB intervals under-cover, they are
calibrated by simulation for this design: the mean bias of $\log\hat k_{B0}$ is subtracted
(\CalBiasForty\%) and the Wald standard error is multiplied by the measured ratio of RMSE to standard
error (\CalInflForty). At 20~dB the uncalibrated coverage is \CalTwentyRawCov{} and no calibration is
applied. Calibrating on three seeds and testing on the other two, the held-out coverage is
\CalSplitACov{} (\CalSplitAk/\CalSplitAn; [\CalSplitALo, \CalSplitAHi]) with the inflation estimated
as \CalSplitAInfl, and \CalSplitBCov{} (\CalSplitBk/\CalSplitBn; [\CalSplitBLo, \CalSplitBHi]) for the
reverse split (inflation \CalSplitBInfl). At 20~dB the held-out coverage without calibration is
\CalTwentySplitACov{} (\CalTwentySplitAk/\CalTwentySplitAn) and \CalTwentySplitBCov{}
(\CalTwentySplitBk/\CalTwentySplitBn; [\CalTwentySplitBLo, \CalTwentySplitBHi]) for the two splits: the
pooled coverage meets the threshold, but it varies between groups of seeds. These intervals are valid
for the simulated design only.

\paragraph{Ignoring the coupling.} Fitting data of the four-wheel coupled model (seed 42, 40~dB,
\IgnoreCouplingN{} datasets) with a single wheelset on the same rail, i.e.\ without the neighbouring
wheels, biases $k_{B0}$ by \IgnoreCouplingForty\,$\pm$\,\IgnoreCouplingFortySE\%, and
\IgnoreCouplingCovered{} of \IgnoreCouplingN{} Wald intervals cover the truth.

\subsection{Multi-wheel coupling}\label{sec:res-multiwheel}
With 60--110~km/h, the bound on $k_{B0}$ is \MWCRBMin--\MWCRBMax\% for one to eight coupled wheels of
the representative wagon (Table~\ref{tab:table5_multiwheel}); the coupling neither helps nor hurts
identifiability (\MWCRBFixedMin--\MWCRBFixedMax\% at a single speed). It does matter for bias
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mwbias}). In a Monte Carlo study with the earlier configuration (a metro wheel
layout, BS113A rail and a $\pm30$-bin kernel), fitting multi-wheel data with a single-wheelset model
biased $k_{B0}$ by \MWSingleBiasTwo\%, \MWSingleBiasFour\% and \MWSingleBiasEight\% for 2, 4 and 8
wheels, while the coupled model gave \MWCorrectBiasTwo\,$\pm$\,\MWCorrectBiasSETwo\%,
\MWCorrectBiasFour\,$\pm$\,\MWCorrectBiasSEFour\% and \MWCorrectBiasEight\,$\pm$\,\MWCorrectBiasSEEight\%.
With the Y25/Eanos layout of the representative wagon, the
four-wheel bias of the single-wheelset model is \IgnoreCouplingForty\% (Section~\ref{sec:res-headline}):
smaller, but still far outside the statistical error. The size of the coupling bias therefore depends
on the wheel layout, and the coupled model is needed in either case.

\subsection{Estimator bias diagnosis}\label{sec:res-bias}
The diagnosis uses the ten seed-123 datasets at 40~dB, where the bias is largest
(\BDBaseFortyBias\% with the $\pm30$-bin kernel, RMSE/bound \BDBaseFortyRatio), and seed~42 as a
contrast (\BDSFtwoFortyBias\%, \BDSFtwoFortyRatio); Table~\ref{tab:table14_validation}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Noise.} The same datasets at 60~dB give \BDBaseSixtyBias\% (RMSE/bound \BDBaseSixtyRatio);
  per dataset the estimates change by at most \BDMaxDiffSNR{} percentage points. The error is fixed by
  the roughness, speed and payload realisation, not by sensor noise.
\item \emph{Scoring and table resolution.} Exact sub-band scoring with a 0.25~Hz table gives
  \BDExactBias\% (at most \BDMaxDiffExact{} percentage points from the default).
\item \emph{Kernel truncation.} Widening the debiasing kernel from $\pm30$ to $\pm120$ bins lowers
  every estimate, to \BDWideBias\% (RMSE/bound \BDWideRatio): about a fifth of the bias. The wide
  kernel is the default.
\item \emph{Number of roughness levels.} With the wide kernel, $K=4$ gives \BDKFourBias\% (RMSE/bound
  \BDKFourRatio); $K=1$ cannot represent the 3~dB sub-band variation of the data and fails
  (RMSE/bound \BDKOneRatio). Too many profiled nuisance levels is not the cause.
\item \emph{Large-sample bias.} With the correct model, pseudo-true fits reproduce relative changes of
  $-2$, $-5$ and $-10$\% with slope \CalSlope.
\item \emph{Correlation between bins.} A sandwich standard error clustered on 61-bin blocks widens the
  40~dB standard error from \RSEModelSEForty\% to \RSEBlockSEForty\% and raises the coverage only from
  \RSEModelCovForty{} to \RSEBlockCovForty{} (fits with the $\pm30$-bin kernel).
\item \emph{Pass design.} Across the 50 headline datasets, speed spread, payload spread, minimum speed
  and the number of bins near the support resonance explain $R^2=\BRegRsq$ of the error (joint
  $p=\BRegP$; payload spread alone $p=\BRegPayloadP$, not significant after four tests).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model-form sensitivity}\label{sec:res-modelform}
Figure~\ref{fig:tornado} and Table~\ref{tab:table6_budget} give the large-sample bias of $k_{B0}$ when
one model parameter is wrong. The unsprung mass dominates (\SensUnsprung\% per $+10$\% mass error),
followed by the foundation stiffness (\SensKf\% per $+10$\%). At realistic $1\sigma$ ranges the
absolute budget is \BudgetNominal\% (root sum of squares), or \BudgetNominalMassOne\% with the
unsprung mass known to $\pm1$\%; re-estimating $k_f$ and $k_{H0}$ with $k_{B0}$ reduces it to
\BudgetCoest\% and \BudgetCoestMassOne\%. The absolute level of $k_{B0}$ is therefore reported only
with this budget; the monitoring output is the relative change.

\subsection{Parameter ranges of the representative vehicle}\label{sec:res-sweep}
Table~\ref{tab:table15_sweep} repeats the bound and the detection limit at the low and high end of the
published (or, where none was found, assumed) range of each parameter, one at a time, with data
generated and fitted by the same model. At the nominal values the support mode is at
\SweepNomMode~Hz, the bound of $k_{B0}$ is \SweepNomCRB\% and the uncalibrated two-period MDC is
\SweepNomMDC\% ($N=50$, 40~dB). Over all ranges except the extension of $k_{B0}$ discussed below, the
bound lies between \SweepCRBMin{} and \SweepCRBMax\%, the MDC between \SweepMDCMin{} and
\SweepMDCMax\% and the support mode between \SweepModeMin{} and \SweepModeMax~Hz.

Two parameters dominate. The first is the primary damping: at the laden value of $c_p$ the MDC is
\SweepCpMDCHi\%, against \SweepCpMDCLo\% at the empty value, because the strongly damped laden
suspension widens the support resonance. The model uses one value of $c_p$ for all payloads; with the
laden damping acting on loaded passes only, the bound for a mixed-payload period lies between the two.
The second is the support stiffness itself: a stiffer support moves the resonance towards the upper
edge of the 700~Hz analysis band (\SweepKBModeHi~Hz and an MDC of \SweepKBMDCHi\% at 3~GN/m), and at
the bearing-only stiffness of 5~GN/m, the upper end of the extended range, the resonance reaches the
band edge (\SweepKBExtModeHi~Hz) and the MDC rises to \SweepKBExtMDCHi\%. A softer support
(0.5~GN/m) lowers the MDC to \SweepKBMDCLo\%.

The empty axle load of 5.8--6.6~t found for Eaos/Eanos wagons, instead of the 5.5~t of the design,
leaves the bound unchanged (\SweepWminCRBLo--\SweepWminCRBHi\% against \SweepNomCRB\%). For the
1400--1526~kg wheelset masses of the multibody models, the bound is \SweepWsWheelsCRBLo--\SweepWsWheelsCRBHi\%
with the additional mass on the wheels and \SweepWsJournalsCRBLo--\SweepWsJournalsCRBHi\% with it
lumped on the journals; in the latter case the axlebox participation moves to a second symmetric mode
near \SweepWsJournalsModeLo~Hz, while the frequency of the \SweepNomMode~Hz mode changes by less
than 2\%. The track parameters ($k_f$, $\eta_f$, $k_{H0}$) change the bound by at most a few hundredths
of a percentage point.

\subsection{Nonlinear Hertzian contact}\label{sec:res-inverse-crime}
Data and estimator otherwise share the linear model, so the Monte Carlo results above contain no error
from the contact law itself. To test this, \ICNLN{} datasets of the headline design (40~dB) were
generated with nonlinear Hertzian contact, $F = C_H[\delta_0+\delta]_+^{3/2}$, solved in the time domain by
a fixed-point iteration with the linear vehicle--track operators, and fitted with the frozen linear
estimator. The same datasets were also generated with linear contact (identical roughness, speeds,
payloads and noise), so that the effect of the nonlinearity is isolated dataset by dataset. With
nonlinear contact the bias of $k_{B0}$ is \ICNLBias\,$\pm$\,\ICNLBiasSE\% and the RMSE \ICNLRMSE\%
(linear contact: \ICLinBias\,$\pm$\,\ICLinBiasSE\% and \ICLinRMSE\%); the paired difference due to
the nonlinearity is \ICDiffMean\,$\pm$\,\ICDiffSE{} percentage points (at most \ICDiffMax{} in any
dataset), small against the detection limit. With the 40~dB simulation calibration the intervals cover
the truth in \ICNLCovCal{} of datasets [\ICNLCovCalLo, \ICNLCovCalHi] (linear contact
\ICLinCovCal). The dynamic contact deflection reaches \ICDeltaRmsMax\% of the static deflection (rms,
worst pass), and brief loss of contact occurs in some empty passes (at most \ICContactLossMax\% of the
samples of a pass). For \ICPairsN{} pairs of periods with no change and nonlinear contact, the
calibrated test raised \ICPairsFACal{} false alarms (95\% interval of the rate \ICPairsFACalLo--\ICPairsFACalHi).

\subsection{Payload-dependent primary suspension}\label{sec:res-primary}
The primary stiffness and damping of the Y25 bogie differ strongly between the empty and the laden wagon
(Table~\ref{tab:table1_parameters}), whereas the model uses one value of each. As a stress test, data
were generated with both varying linearly with axle load between the empty and laden values, and fitted
with fixed values.

\emph{Mixed payloads.} With passes over the whole payload range and the empty values in the fit, the
large-sample bias of $k_{B0}$ is \PDCoestBias\% ($k_f$, $k_{H0}$ re-estimated), but individual periods
are biased by \PDLevelMin{} to \PDLevelMax\%, depending on the payload mix of the period, and the
errors do not cancel between periods: in \PDPairsN{} pairs of periods with no change the apparent change
reached $|z| = \PDPairsAbsZMin$--$\PDPairsAbsZMax$, and \PDPairsFA{} pairs raised a false alarm. The
standard error of the change also grew to \PDPairsSE\%. Mixed-payload pooling with a fixed primary
suspension is therefore not used.

\emph{Payload bands.} With the comparison restricted to one payload band and the estimator using that
band's fixed values (Section~\ref{sec:relative}), the same stress truth gives a large-sample bias of
\BandEmptyPseudo\% (empty band) and \BandLadenPseudo\% (laden band). In \BandEmptyPairsN{} zero-change
pairs in the empty band, $z$ ranged from \BandEmptyZMin{} to \BandEmptyZMax{} with \BandEmptyPairsFA{}
false alarms (95\% interval of the rate \BandEmptyPairsFALo--\BandEmptyPairsFAHi), and the standard error
of the change was \BandEmptySE\%, as without the stress; the apparent changes lean upwards (mean
\BandEmptyMeanD\%, \BandEmptyZPos{} of \BandEmptyPairsN{} positive), which the one-sided test for a loss
of stiffness does not flag. In \BandLadenPairsN{} laden pairs there was no false alarm either; in
\BandLadenNSingular{} of them the observed information was singular because $c_B$ ran to its lower bound,
and with the median standard error used for those, $z$ ranged from \BandLadenZMin{} to \BandLadenZMax{}
(largest apparent change an increase of \BandLadenDMax\%). The detection limits per band, from
the Cram\'er--Rao bound with $k_f$ and $k_{H0}$ re-estimated, are \BandEmptyMDCFiftyMin--\BandEmptyMDCFiftyMax\%
($N=50$) and \BandEmptyMDCTwoHundredMin--\BandEmptyMDCTwoHundredMax\% ($N=200$) for the empty band, and
\BandLadenMDCFiftyMin--\BandLadenMDCFiftyMax\% and \BandLadenMDCTwoHundredMin--\BandLadenMDCTwoHundredMax\%
for the laden band, where the strongly damped laden suspension widens the support resonance; in the laden
band the support damping $c_B$ is not resolved. Payload diversity therefore remains a lever only within
a band, and the empty band is the preferred operating mode.

\subsection{Relative change, interval and detection}\label{sec:res-relative}
A model error that biases $\log\hat k_{B0}$ by a constant cancels in the change $D$; what remains
scales with $D$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:relchange}, Table~\ref{tab:table7_gains}). With $k_f$ and $k_{H0}$
fixed, the gain uncertainty from one-at-a-time pseudo-true fits is $\kappa=\KappaNominal$, dominated by
the foundation stiffness (\KappaKfTerm). On a fresh Monte Carlo with all five model errors drawn at
random it under-covers: \KapNomCov{} [\KapNomLo, \KapNomHi], with an rms error of the change of
\KapNomGainErr{} of $\Delta$, because the gain grows faster than linearly in $k_f$. Re-estimating $k_f$
and $k_{H0}$ in each period lowers the one-at-a-time value to $\kappa=\KappaCoest$; on a fresh Monte
Carlo (\KapCoestN{} replicates, $k_f$ drawn from $\times$\KapCoestKfMin{} to $\times$\KapCoestKfMax)
the interval covered \KapCoestCovered/\KapCoestN{} [\KapCoestLo, \KapCoestHi]. The rms error of the
change was \KapCoestRMS{} of $\Delta$ with a mean of \KapCoestMean{} (the change is under-estimated by
about 6\%); it does not grow with the $k_f$ error. With the 40~dB simulation calibration applied to the
standard error of $D$ (the level bias cancels), coverage stays at \KapCalCovered/\KapCalN{}
[\KapCalLo, \KapCalHi]: the remaining misses are errors in the gain, not in the standard error.

\paragraph{Minimum detectable change.} For a one-sided test at 5\% false alarm and 90\% detection,
the MDC with $k_f$ and $k_{H0}$ re-estimated is \MDCFiftyForty\% for $N=50$ passes per period at
40~dB and \MDCFiftyTwenty\% at 20~dB; with $N=200$ it is \MDCTwoHundredForty\% and
\MDCTwoHundredTwenty\%. With the simulation calibration (40~dB only) these become
\MDCCalFiftyForty\% ($N=50$) and \MDCCalTwoHundredForty\% ($N=200$); 20~dB is unchanged
(Table~\ref{tab:table8_mdc}).

\subsection{Health relevance}\label{sec:res-health}
For the Y25 cylindrical roller bearings under the series reading~\eqref{eq:series} (initial clearance
\ClearanceZero~\textmu m), a 10~\textmu m growth of the diametral clearance lowers the contact stiffness
by \ContactPctTenEmpty\% (empty) and \ContactPctTenLoaded\% (loaded), and the effective support
stiffness by \EffPctTenEmpty\% and \EffPctTenLoaded\% (Table~\ref{tab:table13_health}). The effective
change reaches the detection limit of \HealthMDC\% ($N=50$, 40~dB, co-estimating fit) after
\CylGrowthMDCEmptyNom~\textmu m of growth at empty load and \CylGrowthMDCLoadedNom~\textmu m at full
load. Over the swept initial clearance (0--180~\textmu m), roller count and roller length, the change
per 10~\textmu m ranges from \CylEffTenEmptyMin{} to \CylEffTenEmptyMax\% (empty) and
\CylEffTenLoadedMin{} to \CylEffTenLoadedMax\% (loaded), and the growth that reaches the detection
limit from \CylGrowthMDCEmptyMin{} to \CylGrowthMDCEmptyMax~\textmu m (empty) and
\CylGrowthMDCLoadedMin{} to \CylGrowthMDCLoadedMax~\textmu m (loaded). The changes are not smooth in
the clearance, because the number of rollers in the load zone changes in steps. For the secondary case,
a double-row tapered roller unit with the geometry of~\citep{guan2023}, the corresponding growths are \TapGrowthMDCEmptyMin--\TapGrowthMDCEmptyMax{}
and \TapGrowthMDCLoadedMin--\TapGrowthMDCLoadedMax~\textmu m. Empty passes are therefore the more
sensitive to clearance growth; a mixed-payload period gives a change between the two. The other causes
of support-stiffness loss (fits, housing, adapter, pad) are not modelled here; their stiffness change
enters $k_B$ undiluted.

\subsection{Foundation drift}\label{sec:res-drift}
A seasonal change of the foundation stiffness between periods is the main false-alarm risk
(Table~\ref{tab:table11_drift}). With $k_f$ fixed, a later foundation 10\% or 20\% softer appears as a
change of \DriftSoftTenApparent\% or \DriftSoftTwentyApparent\% in $k_{B0}$ (false-alarm rate
\DriftSoftTenFA{} at 10\%), and a stiffer one masks real losses (MDC \DriftStiffTenMDC\% and
\DriftStiffTwentyMDC\%). Re-estimating $k_f$ and $k_{H0}$ per period keeps the MDC at \DriftCoestMDC\%
for every drift level. A Monte Carlo with the later foundation 20\% softer and no true change
(\DriftMCNomN{} replicates per estimator) confirms this: \DriftMCNomFA/\DriftMCNomN{} false alarms with
$k_f$ fixed ([\DriftMCNomLo, \DriftMCNomHi], mean apparent change \DriftMCNomMean\%) against
\DriftMCCoestFA/\DriftMCCoestN{} with $k_f$ re-estimated ([\DriftMCCoestLo, \DriftMCCoestHi], mean
\DriftMCCoestMean\%).

\subsection{Wheel roughness at rotation orders}\label{sec:res-wheel}
Wheel out-of-roundness puts lines at the rotation orders of an angle-synchronous record
(Table~\ref{tab:table9_wheel_lines}). Removing the order bins (notch) costs about \WLNotchCost\% of the
bound (\WLNone\% without wheel roughness). Modelling them as lines with a mean shared across passes
lowers the bound to \WLTachoZero\% when the lines are at the roughness level and \WLTachoTen\% when
they are 10~dB above it, because the lines then carry information about $|H|$ at known frequencies.

\subsection{Repeated passes over one section}\label{sec:res-repeated}
When passes repeat over the same stretch, their roughness is partially coherent. For coherence
0.5--0.9 with random lateral offsets, the independent-pass estimator stays unbiased (at most
\PCBiasMax\%), and its model standard error is conservative (RMSE/SE \PCModelRatioMin--\PCModelRatioMax);
the bin-clustered sandwich standard error is calibrated (RMSE/SE \PCSandRatioMin--\PCSandRatioMax) and is
used for repeated sections (Table~\ref{tab:table10_partial}).

\subsection{Validation against the P2 failure mode}\label{sec:res-p2}
Carrigan and Talbot~\citep{carrigan2023} estimate the foundation stiffness from the P2 peak of single
passes and call for a reliable way to reject bad estimates. On their simulated setup with a resilient
foundation (Fig.~\ref{fig:p2}, Table~\ref{tab:table12_p2}), corrugation near the P2 frequency puts
\PTwoCTOutsideCorr\% of per-pass estimates outside $\times0.7$--$\times1.43$ of the reference (median
\PTwoCTMedianCorr~MN/m$^2$; \PTwoCTOutsideClean\% without corrugation). The multi-pass likelihood,
fitting $k_f$ instead of $k_{B0}$, gives a median of \PTwoWhMedianCorr~MN/m$^2$ with corrugation and
\PTwoWhMedianClean~MN/m$^2$ without (truth 7.7), but its profile interval covers the truth in only
\PTwoWhCovCorr{} of sections with corrugation (\PTwoWhCovClean{} without).
