\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Residual bias and simulation-calibrated intervals.} With the frozen estimator the estimate
of $k_{B0}$ retains a bias of about \HLFortyBias--\HLTwentyBias\% over all seeds (Table~\ref{tab:table3_headline};
up to \BDWideBias\% for the seed with the largest bias),
and at 40~dB a scatter \HLFortyRatioSE{} times the Wald standard error. Section~\ref{sec:res-bias}
rules out the obvious causes: the large-sample (pseudo-true) bias is nil (slope \CalSlope), sensor
noise does not change the error, exact sub-band scoring and a finer table change it by at most
\BDMaxDiffExact{} percentage points, robust (sandwich) standard errors do not restore coverage, and
fewer roughness levels do not remove it (no incidental-parameter effect). Truncation of the debiasing
kernel explains about a fifth and is removed by the wider kernel. The remainder is unexplained. The
40~dB intervals are therefore simulation-calibrated for the stated design (speed range, payload range,
number of passes, roughness model and noise level), and must be re-calibrated for any other design.
For relative changes under random model errors, the calibrated interval still under-covers
(\KapCalCovered/\KapCalN): the gain uncertainty $\kappa$ derived from one-at-a-time model errors is too
small, and an interval for the change that holds under realistic model errors remains open.

\paragraph{Load exponent as a clearance indicator.} Clearance growth changes not only the level of the
effective support stiffness but also its dependence on axle load, because a larger clearance shrinks
the load zone more at light load. In principle the load exponent $p$ of~\eqref{eq:powerlaw} could
therefore separate clearance growth from other losses of support stiffness. With the exponent free
alongside $(k_{B0}, c_B)$ and payloads spanning \PayloadMin--\PayloadMax~t, the Cram\'er--Rao bound of
$p$ is \ExpCRBFifty{} for $N = 50$ passes and \ExpCRBTwoHundred{} for $N = 200$, and freeing $p$ raises
the bound of $k_{B0}$ from \KBCRBExpFixed\% to \KBCRBExpFree\% ($N=50$). Under the series reading of
the support (placeholder bearing geometry), 10 and 20~\textmu m of clearance growth raise the
effective exponent by only \ExpShiftTen{} and \ExpShiftTwenty{}. Detecting a shift between two
periods (one-sided 5\% false alarm, 90\% detection) needs \ExpMDCFifty{} at $N=50$ and
\ExpMDCTwoHundred{} at $N=200$, so only 20~\textmu m at $N=200$ would be detected. The exponent is
therefore held fixed in the estimator, and the method reports a loss of effective support stiffness
without attributing it to a mechanism.
