\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The vertical support stiffness of a wheelset bearing can be identified from in-service axle-box
acceleration without measuring the rail roughness, by pooling passes at different speeds and payloads
in a debiased Whittle likelihood in which the roughness spectrum levels are nuisance parameters. For a
representative freight wagon on ballasted track, matched-model simulations show that:
\begin{itemize}
\item with 50 passes at 60--110~km/h the matched-model simulation accuracy is a bias of at most
  \HLBiasAbsMax\% and a root-mean-square error of \HLRMSEMin--\HLRMSEMax\%, within \HLRatioCRBMax{}
  times the Cram\'er--Rao bound; intervals at 40~dB are calibrated by simulation for the stated design;
\item the speed spread is the design variable that makes the support stiffness identifiable, and the
  wheel-to-wheel coupling through the rail must be modelled, since ignoring it biases the estimate by
  \IgnoreCouplingForty\%; nonlinear Hertzian contact, by contrast, shifts the estimate by only
  \ICDiffMean{} percentage points;
\item the absolute level carries a model-form uncertainty of about \BudgetCoest\%, so the method is a
  trend monitor: comparing periods within a payload band and re-estimating the foundation and contact
  stiffness in each, a decrease of \BandEmptyMDCFiftyMin--\BandEmptyMDCFiftyMax\% is detectable with 50
  empty passes per period, or \BandEmptyMDCFiftyConsMin--\BandEmptyMDCFiftyConsMax\% allowing for
  simultaneous model errors, and a change of the track foundation between periods causes no bias;
\item periods must be compared within one payload band: pooling passes of mixed payload is not
  supported by the present model, because with a fixed primary suspension the error of each period
  depends on its payload mix and raised false alarms, while within a band it did not;
\item a loss of housing, adapter or pad stiffness reaches the identified stiffness directly, while
  clearance growth is diluted by the stiff roller contacts, so the method reports support-stiffness
  loss without attributing it to a mechanism.
\end{itemize}
These are simulation results: before operational use the method needs a controlled rig or field test with
a known change of support stiffness, which is the next step, followed by co-estimation of a payload-dependent
primary suspension, to restore the full payload range; application to
freight wagons of Indian Railways, once vehicle-specific values are available from RDSO; an extension of
the model to the pinned--pinned frequency band; and calibrated intervals for the track-side use of the
same likelihood.
