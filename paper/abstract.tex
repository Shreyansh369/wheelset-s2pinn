\begin{abstract}
Loss of the vertical support stiffness of a wheelset bearing (loose fits, degraded housing, adapter or
pad, clearance growth) shifts the axlebox resonance, which on freight wagons lies in the band of
axle-box acceleration excited by rail roughness. We pool passes at different
speeds in a single debiased Whittle likelihood: the resonance stays fixed in frequency while the
roughness excitation moves with speed, so the roughness spectrum levels become nuisance parameters. A finite-element wheelset is coupled to neighbouring wheels through an infinite Timoshenko
rail. For a representative freight wagon (Y25 bogies, ballasted 60E1 track) and 50 passes at
60--110~km/h, the matched-model simulation accuracy is a bias of at most \HLBiasAbsMax\% and a
root-mean-square error of \HLRMSEMin--\HLRMSEMax\%, within \HLRatioCRBMax{} times the Cram\'er--Rao
bound. Ignoring wheel-to-wheel coupling biases the estimate by \IgnoreCouplingForty\%; nonlinear
Hertzian contact shifts it by \ICDiffMean{} percentage points. Absolute values carry a model-form
uncertainty of about \BudgetCoest\%, so the method targets changes between periods, and periods must be
compared within one payload band: mixed-payload pooling would need a payload-dependent suspension model and is not supported. In the empty band, re-estimating the foundation and contact stiffness per period, a decrease of \BandEmptyMDCFiftyMin--\BandEmptyMDCFiftyMax\% is detectable from 50 passes per
period (\BandEmptyMDCFiftyConsMin--\BandEmptyMDCFiftyConsMax\% allowing for simultaneous model errors) and
\BandEmptyMDCTwoHundredMin--\BandEmptyMDCTwoHundredMax\% from 200, and a 20\% softer foundation causes no
bias. Clearance growth barely changes the effective stiffness (\EffPctTenEmpty\% per 10~\textmu m), so the
method reports support-stiffness loss not its mechanism. A controlled rig or field test is needed
before operational use.
\end{abstract}
