\begin{abstract}
Loss of the vertical support stiffness of a wheelset bearing, through loosening of the fits, degradation
of the housing, adapter or pad, or growth of the bearing clearance, shifts the resonance of the axlebox
on its support. On freight wagons this resonance lies in the band of axle-box acceleration excited by
rail roughness, but any stiffness identified from that acceleration is confounded with the unknown
roughness. We pool passes at different speeds and payloads in a single debiased Whittle likelihood:
the support resonance stays fixed in frequency while the roughness excitation moves with speed, so the
roughness levels can be left as free nuisance parameters. The forward model is a finite-element
Timoshenko wheelset coupled to neighbouring wheels through an infinite Timoshenko rail. For a
representative freight wagon (Y25 bogies, 22.5~t axle load, ballasted 60E1 track) and 50 passes at
60--110~km/h, simulations give a bias of \HLTwentyBias\% (20~dB) and \HLFortyBias\% (40~dB) and a root-mean-square
error of \HLTwentyRMSE--\HLFortyRMSE\% against a Cram\'er--Rao bound of \HLTwentyCRB\%; ignoring wheel-to-wheel coupling biases
the estimate by \IgnoreCouplingForty\%. Because absolute values carry a model-form uncertainty of
about \BudgetCoest\%, the method targets changes between periods. With the foundation and contact
stiffness re-estimated in each period, a decrease of \MDCCalFiftyTwenty--\MDCCalFiftyForty\% is
detectable from 50 passes per period and \MDCCalTwoHundredTwenty--\MDCCalTwoHundredForty\% from 200,
and a 20\% change of the track foundation raises \DriftMCCoestFA{} false alarm in \DriftMCCoestN{}
trials, against \DriftMCNomFA{} in \DriftMCNomN{} with the track held fixed. Intervals are
simulation-calibrated for the stated design. Clearance growth alone changes the effective stiffness
little (\EffPctTenEmpty\% per 10~\textmu m, empty), so the method reports support-stiffness loss
rather than its mechanism.
\end{abstract}
